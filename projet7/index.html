The Disappearance of Detective Marlowe

Film Noir Short Film

Context

Created as part of a first-year group project at the IUT, The Disappearance of Detective Marlowe is an approximately one-minute short film. The goal was to create an audiovisual production capturing the tropes and aesthetics of film noir, a classic 1950s cinematic genre.

Process

Production & Project Management

We worked as a team of five. While the synopsis was provided by our professor, all subsequent stages were handled entirely by us: filming, video editing, and post-production.

Shooting took place in the IUT's film studio as well as its basement (for the confrontation between Marlowe and the criminal). The set dressing was minimalist and functional: a wooden desk, a chalkboard, scattered newspapers, and period props.

Across the two shooting sessions, I took on versatile responsibilities: during the first session (Marlowe in his detective office), I was in charge of lighting design. During the second session (Marlowe's assassination sequence), I managed on-set audio recording.

Post-production editing was carried out using Adobe Premiere Pro. To achieve the distinctive film noir look, I graded all footage in high-contrast black-and-white and added film grain and dust overlays. I focused heavily on capturing the brooding, suspenseful mood essential to the genre.
