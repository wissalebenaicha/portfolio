Earth Inbox

Awareness Short Film

Context

Produced as part of a group assignment at the IUT, Earth Inbox is a one-minute short film. The goal was to create an advocacy-driven audiovisual piece addressing a contemporary issue. Together with my team, we chose a rarely discussed yet highly relevant topic: the environmental footprint of unemptied email inboxes.

Process

Creative Vision

We chose an absurd, comedic angle to catch viewers off guard while delivering a compelling ecological message. The premise is simple: a teenager continuously ignores system emails telling him to empty his trash bin. He ends up getting violently arrested by a "digital police force." This stark contrast between such a mundane habit and an extreme reaction prompts viewers to reconsider their daily digital behaviors.

Production & Project Management

We worked independently through every stage: drafting the synopsis, writing the director's statement, filming, editing, and distribution.

Filming took place inside a university classroom due to schedule constraints outside class hours. We used a minimalist set—just a computer and screen glow—to intensify the character's sense of digital confinement.

Post-production editing was handled in Adobe Premiere Pro, focusing heavily on sound design to build dramatic tension: mouse clicks, warning alarms, and police sirens...

Key Takeaways

This project allowed me to explore multiple facets of filmmaking within a short timeframe. I realized that straightforward creative decisions (set design, lighting, humor) can generate a powerful impact, especially in cause-driven communication. Furthermore, I discovered how to harness sound design and editing pace to build suspense and captivate an audience.

Above all, I learned how to collaborate effectively in a team by delegating roles according to individual strengths. It also taught me how to champion an environmental message and craft simple, high-impact storytelling through short-form video.
